How funding works

  1. Check eligibility. You must be an African-owned, registered group with at least one year of operating history.
  2. Submit a short concept note. Tell us the problem, your plan and your budget.
  3. Due diligence. We verify your registration, ownership and finances through official channels.
  4. Grant agreement. Funds go to a business account in your organisation's name, in stages tied to milestones.

Our approach

Locally led

We fund African-owned organisations because the people closest to a challenge are best placed to solve it.

Transparent

Our criteria, process and policies are published, and every applicant is told clearly how decisions are made.

Accountable

Grants are paid in stages against agreed milestones, and grantees report progress in plain language.

What we look for in an application

  • A clear problem and a practical plan to address it.
  • A registered, African-owned organisation with a track record of operating.
  • A realistic budget and a way to measure results.
  • A benefit that reaches the community and lasts beyond the grant.

Our policies

Anti-fraud policy

Applying for a grant is always free. We never ask applicants for a processing fee, registration fee, PIN, OTP, password or bank login. Any message asking for these is not from us. Grant payments are made only to a verified organisation account in the grantee's own name.

Privacy policy

The application form collects your organisation name, country, focus area, email, amount requested and project description. We use this only to review your application and reply to you. We do not sell your information. Form submissions are delivered to us through a third-party form service. Please do not include ID numbers, bank details or passwords in your application.

Eligibility and grant policy

Grants are open to African-owned, registered organisations with at least one year of operating history. Awards depend on panel review and due diligence checks, and funds are released in stages tied to agreed milestones. Submitting a concept note does not guarantee funding.
